# Verification: A hot source

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Early tests: Rutherford (with Petavel) heated radon to about 2500 °C in an explosion; Marie Curie and Kamerlingh Onnes cooled radium to about 20 K in 1913, finding less than about 0.1% change: https://arxiv.org/abs/nucl-th/0505021 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.4338 (abstracts).
- Energy ratio "tens of thousands to millions" (keV decays are only about 10⁴ × kT); kT at room temperature 0.025 eV.
- Be-7: about 1%, up to 1.5% at 5 K (PRL 98, 252501, 2007).
- Stripping "heavy" atoms; electron-capture effects tiny but not zero on Earth (Ni-59, Ca-41, K-40 EC branch).
- Th-229 isomer lifetime depends hugely on surroundings; vacuum ultraviolet (148.38 nm); Tiedau et al., PRL 132, 182501 (2024).
- Option wording: "It no longer applies: heating destroys the radioactivity".

## Verified
- Co-60 is β⁻ (not EC), Q = 2.82 MeV; thermal time dilation about 3 × 10⁻¹²; still solid at 1000 °C: derivation.
- Bare Re-187 32.9 years (Bosch et al., PRL 77, 5190, 1996).
- Seasonal claims not confirmed: Pommé et al., Phys. Lett. B 761, 281 (2016): https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC101875
- Chernobyl wildfires (2020) resuspended Cs-137: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-021-04902-7

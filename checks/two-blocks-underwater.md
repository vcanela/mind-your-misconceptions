# Verification: Two blocks underwater

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Overgeneralisation: buoyant force is independent of material only for fully submerged objects (a floating object's buoyant force equals its own weight). Misconception headline reworded to "Heavy objects sink because the water pushes up on them less".
- Galileo's method (La Bilancetta, 1586): compare the apparent loss of weight in water of the crown and pure gold, not an equal-mass weighing: https://www.math.nyu.edu/~crorres/Archimedes/Crown/bilancetta.html
- Force meter demo: blocks must sink (a meter can't hold wood under).
- Submerged buoyant force depends on volume, not shape; typical pine 5 to 6 N.
- Submarines: tanks flooded through vents, blown with compressed air; fish swim bladders for neutral buoyancy at depth.
- Question cue: dropped "exactly" from the stem; correct option's reason no longer echoes it.

## Verified
- 1 L block: 9.8 N push; lead 111 N; steel about 8 times denser than water: derivation.
- Iceberg about 90% submerged: https://usgs.gov/media/images/ninety-percent-iceberg-below-waterline
- Vitruvius sole source of the Eureka story; Galileo called the overflow method crude.
- No buoyancy, bubbles don't rise on the ISS: https://blogs.nasa.gov/ISS_Science_Blog/2015/05/01/space-station-espresso-cups-strong-coffee-yields-stronger-science/
- Distractors documented: Loverude, Kautz and Heron, Am. J. Phys. 71, 1178 (2003).

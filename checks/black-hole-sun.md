# Verification: A black hole for a Sun

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Event horizon defined as the boundary from inside which nothing can get out; the Newtonian escape-speed argument noted as coincidentally giving the right radius.
- ISCO: inside 3 r_s circular orbits are unstable (eccentric orbits can dip lower and return): https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.2386
- Hawking evaporation: 2.1 × 10⁶⁷ yr, but a solar-mass black hole (about 60 nK) is colder than the CMB and currently grows; Hawking radiation undetected: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_radiation
- "Round body" and "star of the same mass" conditions added.
- Classroom comparison moved from 3 km (at the horizon) to 30 km, with a caveat.
- Gas loses orbital motion through friction in a disc.
- Question: the correct option was the only "nothing happens" option and echoed "exactly". Now two "keep orbiting" options (one with the "black holes pull harder" misconception) against two dramatic ones.

## Verified
- r_s(Sun) = 2.95 km, r_s(Earth) = 8.9 mm; Sun's surface gravity 28 g: derivation.
- Earth's orbit unchanged: shell theorem; Birkhoff's theorem in GR: derivation.
- Sgr A* about 4 million solar masses; S2 period 16 years; 2020 Nobel Prize (Genzel, Ghez).
- EHT images: M87* 2019, Sgr A* 2022.
- Spaghettification outside a stellar-mass horizon; gentle at a supermassive one: derivation.
- ISCO = 6GM/c² = 3 r_s: link above.

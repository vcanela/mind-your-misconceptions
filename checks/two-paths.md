# Verification: Two paths

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts, options and figure together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Short circuits: the other branches are starved because nearly all the supply voltage is lost in the supply's internal resistance and wiring, not because a zero-resistance branch "takes everything" (which contradicted the ideal-supply model).
- Question: the 0.5 A option contradicted the stem (equal division would be 1 A each) and duplicated the "same current" idea; replaced with a genuine proportional-reasoning error (100 A). Correct option's "About" hedge removed.
- RCD: compares live and neutral; trips (typically at 30 mA) when current leaks to earth: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual-current_device
- Earthing: the earth wire also holds the case's potential down before disconnection, lowering the voltage across a person.
- Bird on a wire: safe because it touches nothing at a different potential; large birds bridging wire and pole are electrocuted: https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/1341
- Demo values changed to avoid overheating resistors (10 Ω on 10 V dissipates 10 W).
- "Every path between the same two points".

## Verified
- 1 A, 0.01 A, 1.01 A total; I₁/I₂ = R₂/R₁: derivation.
- Disconnection within a fraction of a second (0.4 s for socket circuits under AS/NZS 3000; study-guide source, Standard not opened).
- Figure matches the text.

# Verification: Half Moon

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent; corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Galileo: Venus's phases refuted Ptolemy's model, not Tycho Brahe's geo-heliocentric one: https://catalogue.museogalileo.it/indepth/PhasesVenus.html
- Lamp-and-ball: holding the ball above head height avoids the eclipse; lowering it into the head's shadow shows one.
- Shape argument: the umbra (about 2.7 Moon diameters) can, at one instant, cross at opposite points; reworded to "usually much closer together".
- Earthshine lit by a nearly full (gibbous) Earth; easiest to see at crescent, not quarter.
- "Same phase on the same night" → same phase at any given moment, turned at different angles.
- Crescent is anything less than half; full Moons pass "above or below" the shadow (up to 5° away); the eclipsed Moon "usually" doesn't vanish (Pinatubo 1992); daytime Moon up for about half the daylight hours.

## Verified
- First quarter at 90° elongation; terminator seen edge-on; shadow reaches the Moon only at full: derivation.
- Synodic 29.53, sidereal 27.32 days (1/S = 1/P − 1/Y); inclination 5.1°.
- Anticlockwise orbit seen from above the North Pole; waxing Moon lit on the left from New Zealand: derivation.
- Earthshine; "old Moon in the new Moon's arms" (traditional, Sir Patrick Spens).
- Red eclipsed Moon from refracted sunlight.
- Emoji drawn the northern way: https://gcp.emojipedia.org/waxing-crescent-moon
- Figure: lit halves face the Sun, anticlockwise order of phases: derivation.

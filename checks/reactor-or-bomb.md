# Verification: Reactor or bomb

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together), with rewritten options rechecked; corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Chernobyl "graphite fire" disputed (WNA: "highly unlikely that the graphite itself burned"); now "explosions and the fires that followed": https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/safety-and-security/safety-of-plants/chernobyl-accident
- Generation times: about 10⁻⁴ s (prompt) in water-cooled reactors, about 10⁻³ s in graphite or heavy-water; delayed neutrons make the response take seconds; growth per generation (bomb doubles, reactor a fraction of a percent) added.
- Bomb material: "highly enriched", typically about 90% (HEU begins at 20%; Little Boy about 80%); fast generations come from unmoderated neutrons, not from assembly speed.
- Hydrogen bombs made "a different reaction" partly defensible; that option replaced with a containment misconception.
- Fukushima: the buildings exploded, not the reactors; delayed neutrons released seconds to about a minute after fission; "every power reactor" (pulsed research reactors excepted); Chernobyl prompt-critical surge of about 100 times added.

## Verified
- LWR fuel 3 to 5% U-235; delayed neutron fraction about 0.65%.
- Fukushima hydrogen from cladding-steam reaction; UNSCEAR: radiation health effects among the public unlikely to be discernible; evacuation harms.
- Oklo about 2 billion years ago, about 150,000 years of intermittent operation, self-regulated by water: https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/the-cycle-of-the-natural-nuclear-reactor
- "Critical" means self-sustaining; cooling tower plumes are condensed water.

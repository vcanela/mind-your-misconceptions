# Verification: Flick of a switch

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent; corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Question now asks for the average drift speed (the random speed is about a million m/s, which an advanced student could otherwise defend).
- Random electron speeds: typically about 10⁶ m/s, up to the Fermi speed of 1.57 × 10⁶ m/s.
- Classical thermal speed at 300 K is about 1.2 × 10⁵ m/s: "more than ten times slower" (13 to 15 times).
- AC: two micrometres either side (amplitude), not the full swing.
- Signal speed: "in everyday cables", with a note on long resistive lines (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_factor ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_squares).
- Headlight electrons start within a few billionths of a second; drift time phrased as "drifting at that speed"; 60 Hz in North America.

## Verified
- n(Cu) ≈ 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³; 5 A in 1 mm² gives 0.37 mm/s; 2 m takes about 1.5 h: derivation.
- "Less than a millimetre per second" is the single defensible option for headlight wires of 0.75 to 2.5 mm²: derivation.
- Energy via fields (Poynting): Feynman Lectures II, ch. 27.
- Cat 5e about 0.64 to 0.72 c; fibre about 0.67 c: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_factor
- HFT co-location: https://questdb.com/glossary/colocation
- AC amplitude 2.2 µm for 10 A rms in 1.5 mm²; hair about 90 µm: derivation.

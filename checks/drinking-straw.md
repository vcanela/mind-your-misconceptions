# Verification: Drinking straw

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Low pressure is less air pushing less hard, not nothing; air can push but not pull.
- A vacuum exerts no pressure (forces such as gravity still act across it).
- Suction cups: the rubber's spring-back lowers the pressure; they work inside the pressurised ISS, but not in a vacuum.
- Moon: an open drink boils, cools itself and partly freezes.
- Torricelli was briefly Galileo's assistant (a pupil of Castelli); Galileo blamed the limited "force of the vacuum"; pump makers rather than well diggers: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelista_Torricelli
- Magdeburg hemispheres: date given as the 1650s, usually 1654 (Museo Galileo dates the horses to 1657): https://catalogue.museogalileo.it/indepth/MagdeburgHemispheres.html

## Verified
- 101,325 Pa = 10.1 N/cm²; about 10 tonnes of air per m²; water column 10.3 m; mercury 0.76 m: derivation.
- Capillary rise in a straw a few mm at most: derivation.
- Pascal and Périer, Puy de Dôme, 19 September 1648, a drop of about 8.5 cm.
- Hyperion about 116 m; cohesion-tension mechanism.
- Card over glass, collapsing can, suction cups: work as described.
- Question: one defensible answer, no cues found.

# Verification: Where was the Big Bang?

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Lemaître's 1931 primeval atom was super-dense but cold; the hot early universe came from Gamow, Alpher and Herman in the late 1940s: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.09459
- CMB uniformity of 1 part in 100,000 applies after removing the dipole from our own motion: https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-cosmic-microwave-background.pdf
- Correct option's "since" was a non sequitur; reworded ("all of space, not just one spot").
- Answer cue: three options named a place and only the key didn't; the "centre" option also duplicated the "one point" option. Replaced with the steady-state view (new feedback from standard history: CMB discovered 1965; light-element abundances).
- Balloon demo: measure along the surface.

## Verified
- Hoyle coined "Big Bang" on BBC radio, 28 March 1949, favouring steady state: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.0219
- Lemaître 1927; Hubble 1929 (PNAS 15, 168).
- Age 13.8 Gyr; observable radius about 46 Gly; finite or infinite unknown: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable_universe
- Hubble's law alone can't distinguish explosion from expansion: derivation.
- Bound systems don't expand; TV static includes a small fraction of CMB: derivation.

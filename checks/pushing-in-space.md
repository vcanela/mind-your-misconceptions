# Verification: Pushing in space

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together); corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- "Every spacecraft that has changed course has done it this way": false (gravity assists, solar sails, aerobraking). Now "every rocket engine and thruster works this way": https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter4-1/
- Vacuum thrust gain is noticeable, typically 10 to 25% (Merlin 1D 845 → 914 kN; RS-25 1860 → 2279 kN): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RS-25
- Mechanism: ambient pressure acts on the vehicle's outside, unbalanced across the nozzle exit; it does not push back on supersonic exhaust: https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/rockth.html
- Momentum: conserved throughout, including expelled exhaust.
- NYT: editorial 13 January 1920; correction 17 July 1969 ending "The Times regrets the error": https://www.popsci.com/military-aviation-amp-space/article/2009-07/new-york-times-nasa-youre-right-rockets-do-work-space
- Tsiolkovsky published in 1903 (derived 1897).
- Ion engines: bigger total Δv than an onboard chemical engine with the same propellant; Dawn about 11 km/s: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/blog/2011/3/dear-conndawnsseurs
- Skateboard demo made safe (seated, feet up, ball under about 2 kg, to a catcher).
- Moon: almost no atmosphere (thin exosphere).
- Question: stem no longer contradicts option a; gravity option no longer weakened by "far out in space".

## Verified
- Third-law and momentum account; thrust = ṁv_e + (p_e − p_a)A_e: derivation.
- Vacuum nozzles larger, poor at sea level: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Merlin
- Rocket equation, logarithm, staging: derivation.
- Dawn: about 91 mN, Vesta 2011–12, Ceres 2015–18: JPL link above.

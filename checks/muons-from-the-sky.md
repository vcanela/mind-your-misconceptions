# Verification: Muons from the sky

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together), with rewritten options rechecked; corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Idealisation presented as fact: a muon at 0.995c (about 1 GeV) would stop before the ground, since muons lose about 2 GeV crossing the atmosphere and average about 4 GeV at the ground (PDG): https://pdg.lbl.gov/2020/reviews/rpp2020-rev-cosmic-rays.pdf . Question and numbers rebuilt on realistic speeds (more than 99.5% of c; Lorentz factors of several tens).
- Symmetry: inertial observers' disagreement is not "clocks accumulating different time"; the invariant difference appears when clocks reunite.
- Relativistic Doppler contains time dilation; it is the classical shift that is removed.
- Optical clocks: about 10 m/s and 33 cm (2010); a millimetre-tall atom cloud (2022).
- Frisch and Smith 1962 to 1963; GPS ranging errors about 10 km a day; Hafele–Keating agreement within uncertainty.
- Options: "really" removed (paired with "only appear"); low-altitude option (contradicted the stem) replaced with "only the long-lived ones arrive", refuted by the numbers; "the few" removed (contradicted "large numbers").

## Verified
- γ(0.995c) = 10; 15 km takes 50 µs; e^(−22.7) ≈ 10⁻¹⁰: derivation.
- Production about 15 km; flux about 1 per cm² per minute: PDG link above.
- GPS −7 + 45 = 38 µs/day; Rossi and Hall 1941; Frisch and Smith; Bailey et al., Nature 268, 301 (1977), γ = 29.33: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_testing_of_time_dilation

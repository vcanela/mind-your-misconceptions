# Verification: Two power supplies

Checked 2026-10-09 by an independent fact-checking agent (facts and options together), with the redesigned question rechecked; corrections applied the same day.

## Corrected
- Question redesigned. The original (car battery against a wall socket "protected by a 10 A fuse") was wrong for New Zealand (sockets rated 10 A, circuits on 16 to 20 A breakers) and didn't test the misconception, since "sockets are dangerous" is everyday knowledge. Now a 12 V battery delivering up to 500 A against a 230 V AC supply limited to 0.2 A, gripped hand to hand. Id renamed from battery-or-socket.
- Skin resistance falls steeply above about 50 V; complete breakdown only above about 450 to 600 V; hand to hand at mains about 1 to 2 kΩ (IEC 60479 table): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_shock ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2763825/
- Mains shocks cause fibrillation, not a stopped heart; "can be lethal" rather than "easily".
- Fibrillation thresholds of tens of mA are for hand-to-feet; hand to hand (heart-current factor 0.4) is about 100 to 125 mA.
- "What the supply can deliver doesn't matter" contradicted the Van de Graaff and fence explanations; reworded.
- Car battery dangers: shorting burns, hydrogen and acid; electric-vehicle traction batteries can kill: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/225520/cdc_225520_DS1.pdf
- Defibrillator 150 to 200 J in about 10 ms, across the chest: https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/ems/resources/clinical-information/biphasic_clinical_tech_overview.pdf
- Van de Graaff: microamp belt current, under 1 J, exclude pacemaker and heart-condition users: https://spark.iop.org/van-de-graaff-generator-safety
- ELV wording "not exceeding ... ripple-free"; classroom body resistance 5 kΩ for a firm dry grip at 12 V.

## Verified
- AC perception about 1 mA, let-go about 10 to 16 mA: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_injury
- 230 V / 1.35 kΩ ≈ 170 mA: derivation.
- ELV 50 V AC / 120 V ripple-free DC (AS/NZS 3000; secondary source).
- Static shocks 1.5 to 35 kV; electric fence pulses about 8 to 10 kV, ≤ 10 ms, ≤ 5 J (IEC 60335-2-76).
- RCDs can't detect live-to-neutral contact: https://www.pdl.co.nz/getmedia/0e4286a6-5834-485e-bcc1-2938bde0bca5/RCD-protection-installation-guide.pdf
